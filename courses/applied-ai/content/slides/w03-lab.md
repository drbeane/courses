## Practice: Find the Problems

- Your handout has five AI answers. Some are subtly wrong; some may be fine.
- For each one, decide: right, wrong, or can't tell.
- Write down **how you know**: the check you ran, the source you found, or what made you suspicious.
- Work alone first, then compare with a neighbor.


---

## Debrief

- Which errors did you catch, and what tipped you off?
- Which did you miss, and why? Was it the tone, the topic, or the time it would have taken?
- What was your fastest check?
- Which of the six error types was each problem?

## The Lab

1. Choose a topic you know well, so you can recognize a wrong answer.
2. Try to get a confident wrong answer from an AI tool.
3. Document:
   - the prompt
   - the output
   - **how you detected** the error
   - how you fixed it (prompt, model, source)

---

## Strategies That Tend to Work

- Ask for specific citations, with authors, titles, and page numbers.
- Ask about something local or recent, such as a campus office or this month's news.
- Ask for exact figures: populations, prices, dates.
- Lead the question by building a false assumption into it.
- Ask for a long chain of reasoning, where one bad step can carry through.

## Responsible Use

- You're responsible for what you submit or send, **whoever drafted it**.
- Disclose AI assistance, as the course policy requires.
- "The AI said so" is not a source. Cite the source you checked, not the chat.
- When you can't verify a claim, leave it out or label it as unverified.

These apply to every assignment from here on, not just this one.
