## A Working Definition of AI

**Artificial intelligence**: a subfield of computer science that is interested in building computer systems that do tasks we would call intelligent if a person did them.

- Understanding language, recognizing images, predicting, planning
- The boundary keeps moving: once a task is routine, people stop calling it AI


---

## Rules, Then Data

- AI is a broad field: expert systems, search algorithms, genetic algorithms, learned models
- First decades: rules written by people. Brittle and slow to build
- Since about 2010: learning from data. More robust, sometimes better

---

## Nested Terms

- **AI**: the whole field
  - **Machine learning**: learns from data
    - **Deep learning**: large neural networks
      - **Generative AI**: creates new text, images, audio, code
        - **Large language models**: generate text from language

This course covers one narrow slice of AI: the part that matters most today.

In this course, "AI tools" means applications built on large language models.

---

## AI You Already Use

- Recommendations in feeds and stores
- Maps and traffic predictions
- Phone cameras and photo search
- Speech recognition and captions
- Fraud alerts and autocomplete

Most of it is narrow: one job each. A spam filter *classifies*. A chat assistant *creates*, across a wide range of tasks.

---

## What "AI" Doesn't Tell You

Ask instead:

1. What does it actually do? Classify, predict, recommend, or generate?
2. What was it built on? Whose data, how current?
3. What happens when it's wrong?
