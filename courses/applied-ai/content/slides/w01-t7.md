## Check These Today

## Turn Off Training in Claude

Personal accounts: Free, Pro, Max

1. Select your name
2. **Settings**
3. **Privacy**
4. Turn off **Help Improve our AI models**

Same steps in the browser, desktop app, and phone app. The setting follows your account, so you only change it once.


---

## Turn Off Training in ChatGPT

Personal accounts: Free, Plus, Pro

1. Account menu (on a phone: sidebar, then your profile icon)
2. **Settings**
3. **Data controls**
4. Turn off **Improve the model for everyone**

Signed out? The choice is saved only in that browser, so it has to be set again on each device.


---

## What Opting Out Does Not Do

- Your chats are **still stored** on the vendor's servers until you delete them.
- Training that already happened is **not undone**.
- Rating a reply with a thumbs up or thumbs down can **opt that chat back in** for training.
- Chats flagged by automated checks still go to **safety review**, where people may read them.

Opting out limits later use. It does not make the text private.

## Chats That Leave No History

- **Claude: Incognito chat**. Not saved to your history and not used for training.
- **ChatGPT: Temporary chat**. Not in your history, doesn't use or create memories, not used for training, and kept up to 30 days for safety review.

Good for a one-off question you don't want saved. Not for work you need to return to, because the chat is gone when you close it.

---

## Personal vs Institutional Accounts

- Business, enterprise, and education plans **don't train on your content by default**.
- The organization sets how long chats are kept and who can access them.
- An administrator may be able to review your use, so a work account isn't private from your employer or school.
- Know which account you're signed in to before you paste anything sensitive.

---

## Never Paste

- Other people's private data, such as names with health, financial, or contact details
- Student records, which are protected by FERPA
- Employer or client confidential information
- Passwords, API keys, and account numbers

When in doubt, leave it out. You can usually describe the problem with made-up or removed details instead.
