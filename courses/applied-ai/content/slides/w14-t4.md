## Workforce Effects

- Tasks change before jobs do: most jobs keep their title while the work inside them shifts.
- Entry-level work is most exposed to automation, and it's also the work people learn from.
- New work is appearing: specifying, supervising, and evaluating AI output.
- Those are the skills this course has practiced since week 1.

---

## A Job Is a Bundle of Tasks

| Moving toward AI | Staying with people |
|---|---|
| First drafts | Deciding what to write, and for whom |
| Summarizing | Judging whether the summary is right |
| First-pass analysis | Choosing the question, owning the conclusion |
| Routine code | Specifying, reviewing, owning what ships |

The right-hand column is this course.


---

## For Your First Years of Work

- **Learn the underlying work**, even where a tool could do it. You can't check what you don't understand.
- **Build expertise in a field**: discernment depends on knowing what right looks like.
- Take on the tasks AI is weak at: judgment calls, relationships, unusual problems.
- Keep learning the tools. They'll change several times before your first promotion.

---

## What a Good AI-Use Policy Contains

- **Scope**: who and what it covers
- **Approved tools** and account types
- **Data rules**: what may and may not go in
- **Disclosure**: when to say AI was used
- **Accountability**: who signs off
- **Review**: when the policy gets updated

---

## What Usable Wording Looks Like

> Client financial records may be used only with tools marked "client data approved". Social Security numbers and bank account numbers may not be entered into any AI tool.

> The staff member who signs a piece of work is responsible for its accuracy, however it was produced.

Says what you **may** do. Names data by level. Names a person.

---

## Common Policy Mistakes

- Banning everything (people use it anyway)
- Naming specific products (out of date in months)
- No data rules
- No owner
- Vague wording ("use AI responsibly")
- Rules nobody can follow

A ban moves use onto personal accounts, out of sight.

---

## Test Your Draft

Read it as:

- a **new employee** on day one, who doesn't know the unwritten rules
- someone **in a hurry** with a client file open, who will skim it
- someone **looking for a loophole**, who will read it literally

Then try it on two real cases from your final project organization. Does it give a clear answer for each?

---

## Your Final Project Includes a Policy

Write a 1-page **draft AI-use policy** for the organization in your final project.

- Cover the six parts: scope, approved tools, data rules, disclosure, accountability, and review.
- Write for the people who'll follow it, in plain language they can act on.
- Test it against the three readers and two real cases before you submit it.
