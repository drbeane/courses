### Week 6

---

## Why an Agent Needs a Brief

- It works for a **long time** before you see anything, so you can't correct it after each step.
- It **acts**: a misunderstanding doesn't just produce a bad answer, it renames 200 files.
- It makes **many small decisions** on its own: which files count, what to name things, what to skip.

Brief it like a capable contractor who has never worked with you and can't call to ask.

---

## The Task Brief

- **Goal**: what done looks like, and why you want it
- **Inputs**: which files and sources to use, and which to ignore
- **Constraints**: what not to touch, where results go, format, and length
- **Acceptance criteria**: how you'll judge the result

A good brief is the difference between a useful result and an hour of cleanup.

---

## Goal and Inputs

**Goal**: the end result **and the reason**
> One combined spreadsheet of 2025 expenses, so I can total them by category for my tax return.

**Inputs**: what to use, and what to leave out
> The twelve CSV files in `statements/`. Ignore `old/`.

The reason lets the agent make sensible calls you didn't anticipate, such as how to handle a refund.

---

## Constraints

- What not to touch: other folders, original files, anything personal
- Where to put results, and how to name them
- Format and length of what it produces
- **What to do when unsure**: stop and ask, or choose, record the choice, and keep going

Without that last line, the agent guesses silently, and you find out at the end.

---

## Acceptance Criteria

| Vague | Checkable |
|---|---|
| "Well organized" | "Every file is in exactly one course subfolder. None remain in the top folder." |
| "Accurate" | "Every figure appears in a source file, and the report names the file." |

Could someone else look at the result and say yes or no?


---

## Weak and Strong

> Organize my downloads folder.

> **Goal.** Find any course file in under a minute.
> **Inputs.** The top level. Leave `personal/` alone.
> **Constraints.** Delete nothing. One subfolder per course. If unsure, use `unsorted/`.
> **Acceptance.** Same file count before and after. `index.md` lists every file.

---

## How Much to Specify?

- Too little, and the agent guesses, often reasonably but not the way you wanted.
- Too much, and you've done the work yourself and written it up as instructions.
- **Specify outcomes and constraints, not every step.** Let the agent work out the how.

Ask for a plan first, and ask the agent what's unclear in your brief before it starts.

---

## When Not to Delegate

- You can't check the result, so you'd have to trust it blindly.
- The data is sensitive, or belongs to someone who didn't agree to share it.
- Mistakes would be costly and hard to undo.
- The point of the task is for you to do it.
- Writing the brief would take longer than doing the task.
