A tokenizer cuts text into pieces taken from a fixed list, its vocabulary. Type some text and see how three small tokenizers split it. They split the same text differently, as different models do.

Three Ways to Split It

Each colored box is one token. A dot (·) marks a space at the front of a token. Real tokenizers usually attach the space to the word that follows it.

How the Large Vocabulary Tokenizer Chooses Pieces

At each step the tokenizer looks at the text that is left and takes the longest piece from its vocabulary that matches the start. If nothing matches, it takes a single character.

What to Notice

  1. Common words are usually one token in the large vocabulary and several in the small one. The count changes with the tokenizer even though the text does not.
  2. Rare words, names, and typos break into many small pieces. Try "Supercalifragilistic" or a misspelled word.
  3. Numbers, code, and text in other languages usually take more tokens than plain English of the same length.
  4. The model never sees the letters inside a token, only its ID. That is why questions about spelling and letter counts are hard for it.

These are teaching tokenizers with short hand-made vocabularies. Real tokenizers have tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of pieces, and no real model will split text exactly as shown here.
