# AI Inside an Application
### Week 5
COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials

---

## Embedded Copilot

AI **inside the app you're already using**.

- On Maryville accounts, that means **Copilot in Outlook**.
- Its strength is that it's where the work is: no copying and pasting between windows.
- Its limit is that it mostly sees only that app's context: the open email, document, or sheet.

---

## What It's Good At

| App | Typical tasks |
|---|---|
| Email | Summarize a thread, draft a reply |
| Documents | Rewrite a passage, summarize |
| Spreadsheets | Explain or write a formula |
| Slides | First deck from a document |
| Meetings | Decisions and action items |

Short tasks on one item that's already open.

---

## Why It's Convenient

- No copying and pasting between the app and a chat window.
- The context is supplied for you: the thread, the document, the selected cells.
- The result lands where it's needed, ready to edit and send.
- It's usually covered by your organization's data agreement, so work data stays under work terms.

---

## What It Can't See

- It mostly sees **this** thread, document, or sheet, and little beyond it.
- It doesn't know the plan in another file, or what was said at last week's meeting.
- The app chooses the model, and you usually can't change it.
- Which features you get depends on your organization's license.

---

## A Button Is a Short Prompt

"Draft reply" → generic.

Type instead:

> Draft a reply that declines the Thursday meeting, proposes Monday or Tuesday afternoon, and keeps it to three sentences.

The button is a one-line prompt someone else wrote. Everything from week 2 still applies when you type your own.

---

## Inside the App or a Separate Assistant?

| Inside the app | Separate assistant |
|---|---|
| Quick tasks on what's in front of you | Work across many sources |
| No setup | Projects, files, agents |
| Limited to that app | Broader tools and models |

Many people use both: the copilot for quick tasks, a separate assistant for anything bigger.

---

## Which Would You Use?

- Catching up on a 30-message thread
- A proposal drawing on three reports and a budget
- A formula that returns an error
- A messy dataset to clean and analyze

For each, ask: where does the information live, and how much of it does the task need?

## Check Before It Goes Out

- A summary can miss the one message that matters, such as a changed deadline.
- A drafted reply can make **commitments** you didn't intend, like agreeing to a date.
- The tone can be wrong for the recipient: too casual for a dean, too stiff for a teammate.

Your name is on it.
