# The 4D Framework
### Week 1
COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials

---

## Four Skills of AI Fluency

From the AI Fluency Framework (Dakan and Feller, 2025):

- **Delegation**: deciding what to hand to AI at all, and how much of it
- **Description**: communicating the task, the context, and what good looks like
- **Discernment**: judging what comes back, and how the AI got there
- **Diligence**: taking responsibility for the result and how it was made

Each week of the course emphasizes one of the four.

---

## A Sequence, and a Loop

Decide → describe → judge → answer for it

- The four skills follow the order of a task: you decide, describe it, judge the result, and stand behind it.
- In practice they form a loop. What you find when you judge usually sends you back to describe the task better.
- Each skill returns all term, applied to more capable tools that need more careful supervision.

---

## This Week: Delegation

Before you open a chat, ask four questions:

- Is this a task AI is good at, or one where it's known to struggle?
- What would a wrong answer cost, and who would pay that cost?
- Can I check the result, and do I know enough to spot an error?
- Should anyone else's data be involved, and do I have permission to share it?

A task that is both costly **and** uncheckable deserves the most thought before you hand it over.

## Try the Questions

| Task | Decision |
|---|---|
| Brainstorm names for a study group | Delegate freely |
| Explain a concept from lecture | Delegate, then confirm |
| Summarize a classmate's paper for peer review | Read it yourself |
| Work out a medication dose | Ask a pharmacist |
| Write this course's reflection essay | Don't delegate |

The same tool gets a different answer for each task, because the cost of an error and the purpose of the task differ.

## What Is the Task For?

If its purpose is for you to **learn**, **practice**, or give **your own judgment**,

a tool that does it for you has removed the purpose.

- A practice problem exists so you build the skill, not so the answer exists.
- A peer review exists because the author wants *your* reading.
- In those cases, AI can still quiz you, explain, or give feedback on your draft.

---

## Delegation Comes in Degrees

- You do the work, and AI **reviews** it.
- You and AI work **together**, trading ideas back and forth.
- AI **drafts**, and you revise.
- AI does the work, and you **check** it.
- AI does it **alone**, and nobody looks before it's used.

The more you hand over, the more checking you owe. Most good uses sit in the middle of this list.

---

## Why a Framework

The tools will have new names by the time you graduate. Some of today's products won't exist.

The four questions still work on a tool you've never seen:

- What should I hand it? How do I tell it what I need?
- How do I judge what it gives me? What am I responsible for?
