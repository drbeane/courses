# The Final Project
### Week 10
COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials

---

## Final Project: The Big Picture

Design, prototype, and evaluate an **AI-supported workflow** for a real or realistic task in your field.

- Choose the tools and surfaces, and justify the choice.
- Build a working prototype, not just a description of one.
- Write an evaluation plan, run it, and report the results.
- Address risk and governance: what could go wrong, and who's accountable.

---

## Final Project Milestones

| Week | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 10 | **Proposal** (1 page) |
| 12 | Checkpoint (status update) |
| 14 | Draft, peer review, AI-use policy |
| 15 | Presentation and final report |

Each milestone is a chance to narrow the scope. Most strong projects end up smaller than they were proposed.

---

## Choosing a Task

- **Recurs**: it happens weekly or monthly, so a workflow pays off.
- Has a **real user**: you, a coworker, a club, a small business.
- Has **checkable results**: you can tell a good output from a bad one.
- **Fits** about four weeks of work alongside your other courses.
- **Safe** to prototype: no one else's private data, nothing that acts on real accounts without approval.

Pick something you know from the inside.

---

## Size It Right

| Too vague | About right |
|---|---|
| "Use AI to help with marketing." | "Turn the store's new-arrivals spreadsheet into three draft posts each week, for the owner to approve." |
| "AI for nursing students." | "Turn one lecture's notes into practice questions, checked against the chapter." |

Small, built, and measured beats ambitious and described.

---

## The Proposal (Due This Week)

1 page:

- The **problem**: what task, and what's wrong with how it's done now
- The **users**: who does it, and who uses the result
- **Tools and surfaces** you'll use, and why those
- What **success** looks like, in terms you can measure

## What Success Looks Like

| Vague | Measurable |
|---|---|
| "The posts will be good." | "The owner approves 2 of 3 drafts with minor edits, for four weeks." |
| "It will save time." | "Under 15 minutes, down from an hour." |

What is counted, the target, and who judges.

You don't have to hit the target. You have to measure against it.
