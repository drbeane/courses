# 06-01 — The Physical Footprint of AI

**Module:** 6 — Environmental Costs and Global Access
**Estimated time:** 35 minutes

**In this lesson:** "The cloud" sounds weightless. It is made of mines, chip factories, enormous buildings, power plants, water and discarded hardware. This lesson traces that physical chain, explains why AI broke an earlier pattern of efficient growth, and shows why the estimates of its footprint vary so widely.

## A Country-Sized Footprint

In June 2026, researchers at the United Nations University's Institute for Water, Environment and Health published estimates of the global footprint of data centers, which Reuters summarized.

| | 2025 (estimate) | 2030 (projection) |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | 448 terawatt-hours, more than Saudi Arabia uses; AI about a fifth of it | 945 terawatt-hours, about what Japan uses; AI about 40 percent |
| Water | 4.5 trillion liters | 9.3 trillion liters |
| Carbon dioxide | 189 million tonnes | 399 million tonnes |
| Land | 6,900 square kilometers | More than 14,500 square kilometers |

The lead author, Kaveh Madani, stressed a point that is easy to miss in numbers like these. The world will not "run out" of water or power because of AI. The concern is local: rapid, poorly planned growth in particular places can collide with limits that are already tight there.

The International Energy Agency's 2025 report *Energy and AI* gave a similar picture. Data centers used about 415 terawatt-hours in 2024, about 1.5 percent of the world's electricity, and that figure was projected to roughly double by 2030, with AI as the main driver.

These are estimates and projections, and the methods behind them matter, as Lesson 06-04 explains.

## "The Cloud" Is Made of Things

AI depends on a physical chain with six links.

**Minerals.** Chips, servers and power infrastructure need copper, lithium, cobalt, gallium, germanium, rare earth elements and high-purity quartz. Mining them has environmental and human costs, from cobalt mines in the Democratic Republic of Congo to rare-earth refining in China.

**Chip fabrication.** The most advanced chips are made in a few factories, mostly operated by TSMC in Taiwan. These factories use large amounts of ultrapure water, energy and specialized chemicals, including some "forever chemicals" that persist in the environment. The emissions from manufacturing a chip, called **embodied carbon**, can be a significant share of its lifetime footprint.

**Data centers.** These are buildings full of servers, networking equipment, cooling systems and power infrastructure. A **hyperscale data center**, the kind run by the largest cloud companies, can draw anywhere from 100 megawatts to more than a gigawatt, comparable to a small city.

**Energy.** Data centers draw power from the grid, from gas turbines built on site (Lesson 06-03 describes one case), and from renewable and nuclear power bought under contract, including deals in 2024 and 2025 to restart nuclear plants that had been shut down.

**Water.** Many data centers cool their equipment by evaporating water, which consumes it. More water is used indirectly to generate the electricity.

**Electronic waste.** AI hardware is replaced quickly, often every three to five years. One 2024 study projected that generative AI could add between 1.2 and 5 million tonnes of electronic waste by 2030.

## What It Supports

It is worth remembering what this infrastructure is for. The same data centers that run chatbots also run hospital records, payment systems, scientific computing, weather forecasts, video calls and the search engines people use to find everything else. Lesson 06-05 describes uses of AI that help protect the climate and the environment. The physical footprint is the cost side of a ledger that also has a benefit side, and the question is which uses are worth what they cost.

## Why AI Broke the Pattern

Between 2010 and 2018, the amount of computing done in the world's data centers grew enormously while their energy use rose only modestly. A 2020 study in *Science* attributed this to steady gains in efficiency. AI broke that pattern for four reasons.

AI runs on accelerated hardware, such as graphics processors, that draws far more power per server rack than ordinary computing: often 40 to more than 130 kilowatts, compared with 5 to 15 for traditional racks. The scale of investment is unprecedented, with the largest technology companies spending hundreds of billions of dollars a year on AI infrastructure by 2025 and 2026. Billions of queries a day add up, as Lesson 06-02 explains. And AI data centers want constant, reliable power, which in practice can mean power from fossil fuels.

The companies' own reports show the effect. Google reported in 2024 that its emissions had risen 48 percent since 2019, largely because of data-center energy. Microsoft reported a rise of about 29 percent since 2020. Both companies point to record purchases of clean energy and argue that AI will help reduce emissions elsewhere. Critics point out that the companies rely on accounting methods that credit those purchases, a question Lesson 06-04 takes up.

## Why the Estimates Vary

Estimates of AI's footprint vary widely, for four reasons.

Companies disclose little. They report total energy use but rarely separate AI from their other computing.

Key shares have to be estimated, such as how much energy goes to training models versus running them, and how much goes to AI versus other work.

Projections depend on things that are highly uncertain: the supply of chips, the availability of power, future gains in efficiency, and demand.

And some planned data centers will never be built. One research firm estimated in 2026 that 30 to 50 percent of the capacity expected to come online that year could be delayed.

**1. What are the six links in AI's physical chain?**

:::answer Answer
Minerals, chip fabrication, data centers, energy, water and electronic waste.
:::

**2. What is embodied carbon?**

:::answer Answer
The emissions from manufacturing hardware, such as chips, as distinct from the emissions from running it.
:::

**3. Why did AI break the earlier pattern of computing growing with little rise in energy use?**

:::answer Answer
AI hardware draws far more power per rack, investment is enormous, billions of daily queries add up, and AI data centers want constant power, which can mean fossil fuels.

## Key Terms

- **Hyperscale data center:** A very large data center operated by one of the major cloud companies.
- **Embodied carbon:** The emissions from manufacturing hardware and building facilities.
- **Rack power density:** The power drawn by one rack of servers.
- **Electronic waste:** Discarded electronic equipment.
