# 02-07 — Always-On Assistants: Voice, Screen, and Memory

**Module:** 2 — Surveillance, Biometrics, and Privacy
**Estimated time:** 40 minutes

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe how always-on AI assistants collect data: wake words, screen capture, persistent memory and ambient context.
2. Analyze specific privacy failures and design changes from 2019 to 2026.
3. Evaluate the trade-off between assistant usefulness and privacy, including effects on non-users.

## Opening case: the setting that disappeared

In March 2025, Amazon emailed Echo owners that it was discontinuing the "Do Not Send Voice Recordings" option. That setting had let certain Echo devices process voice requests locally, so audio never left the home. With the launch of Alexa+, Amazon's generative AI assistant, every voice request would be sent to Amazon's cloud. Amazon said the change was needed to power the new AI features. Users could still choose not to have recordings *saved*.

The more capable the assistant, the more data it needs and the more of it leaves your device.

## Core ideas

### Four modes of "always-on"

| Mode | What is captured | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Wake-word listening** | Audio, buffered locally until the wake word; then streamed | Alexa, Google Assistant, Siri |
| **Screen and activity capture** | Periodic screenshots or activity logs, made searchable | Microsoft Recall; "rewind"-style apps |
| **Persistent memory** | Facts about you that the assistant stores across conversations | ChatGPT memory; Claude memory; Gemini personal context |
| **Ambient sensing** | Continuous audio, video or photos of your surroundings | AI pendants, always-listening glasses (02-08) |

### What has gone wrong

- **Human review of recordings (2019):** Bloomberg, The Guardian and others revealed that contractors for Amazon, Apple, Google and Microsoft listened to voice-assistant recordings, including accidental activations capturing intimate moments, medical details and crimes. Companies paused programs and made human review opt-in.
- **False activations:** Studies found smart speakers wake mistakenly several times a day from TV dialogue (Dubois et al., 2020).
- **Retention of children's data:** In 2023, the FTC fined Amazon $25M for retaining children's Alexa recordings, and some transcripts, indefinitely under COPPA, even after parents requested deletion.
- **Microsoft Recall (2024):** The announced feature would screenshot the user's screen every few seconds and make it AI-searchable. Security researchers showed that the first design stored data in an unencrypted local database readable by malware. Microsoft withdrew it, then relaunched it as opt-in with encryption and Windows Hello authentication (2025).
- **Memory surprises:** Users discovered assistants remembered things they'd shared once, or saw remembered facts resurface in unexpected contexts. Vendors added memory controls and "temporary chat" modes.

### Why AI assistants intensify privacy risk

1. **Context is the product.** An assistant is useful because it knows your calendar, email, contacts, location, habits and history.
2. **Unstructured data becomes searchable.** Recordings and screenshots, once inert, can now be summarized and queried.
3. **Inference.** An assistant with months of data can infer health, relationships, finances and mood (02-09).
4. **Third parties.** Your assistant records guests, callers, children and co-workers, none of whom consented.
5. **Agents act on data.** Assistants that read email and take actions can leak data through prompt injection (Lesson 07-06).
6. **Legal access.** Stored data can be subpoenaed. Smart-speaker recordings have been sought in U.S. criminal cases (e.g., an Arkansas murder case in 2016–2017).

### Design choices that matter

| Choice | More private | Less private |
|---|---|---|
| Where processing happens | On-device | Cloud |
| Retention | Ephemeral; auto-delete | Indefinite |
| Defaults | Off / opt-in | On / opt-out |
| Human review | None or opt-in | Default |
| Training use | Excluded by default | Included by default |
| Memory | Visible, editable, scoped | Hidden or global |
| Indicators | Clear, hardware-enforced lights or sounds | Software-controlled, or none |
| Encryption | End-to-end, or with user-held keys | Provider-held keys |

Apple's "Private Cloud Compute" (2024) is a notable attempt to process requests in the cloud with verifiable privacy guarantees: stateless processing, no retention and independent inspection. Its guarantees depend on the hardware and on audit claims.

### Usefulness vs. privacy

Being useful requires context, so some privacy loss is inherent. The ethical questions are:

- Is data collection **proportionate** to the benefit the user chose?
- Does the user **understand** and **control** what is kept?
- Are **non-users** protected?
- Is the data used for the **user's** purposes, or for the company's (ads, training)?

## Applying the frameworks

- **Contextual integrity:** A conversation at your kitchen table flowing to a cloud server and a contractor violates domestic norms.
- **Autonomy:** Settings that change unilaterally, as with Amazon's in 2025, undermine user control.
- **Justice:** Households that can't afford privacy-preserving devices may get the most data-hungry ones.
- **Decency, others lens:** Guests and children can't consent.

## Discussion questions

1. Was Amazon's 2025 change justified by the improved capability of Alexa+? What would a decent company have offered instead?
2. Would you enable a feature like Recall? Under what conditions?
3. Should AI assistant memory be **on** or **off** by default? Defend your answer, then argue the opposite.
4. Do guests in your home have a right to know about, and refuse, recording by your devices?

## Activity: assistant privacy audit (15 minutes)

Choose one assistant you use (voice, chat or OS-integrated). Find and record:

1. where your data is processed,
2. how long it's retained,
3. whether it's used for training, and how to opt out,
4. what memory exists and how to see or delete it, and
5. whether human review happens.

Rate how easy each was to find (1 = buried, 5 = obvious).

## Key terms

- **Wake word:** The phrase that triggers an assistant to start streaming audio.
- **On-device processing:** Computation done locally, without sending data to servers.
- **Persistent memory:** Information an assistant retains across sessions.
- **Ephemeral processing:** Data processed and discarded without storage.
