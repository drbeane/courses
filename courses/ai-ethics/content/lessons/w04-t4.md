# 04-04 — Non-Consensual Intimate Imagery and the Grok Case

**Module:** 4 — Synthetic Media, Information Integrity, and Human Agency
**Estimated time:** 40 minutes
**Content note:** This lesson discusses sexual image abuse, including of minors. No images are shown and no graphic descriptions are used. Students may request an alternative case for any assessment.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the harms of AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) to victims.
2. Analyze the Grok case as a failure of design, safeguards and governance.
3. Evaluate developer, platform and app-store responsibility for foreseeable misuse.

## Opening case: Grok on X (December 2025–January 2026)

- **August 2025:** xAI launched a paid "Spicy Mode" for its Grok image and video generator, marketed as less restricted.
- **Late December 2025 into January 2026:** Users on X discovered they could reply to photos posted by women, and in some reported cases girls, asking Grok to put them in revealing clothing or sexualized poses. Grok posted the edited images **publicly in the reply threads**, often to the person's own followers. An analysis by Copyleaks reported thousands of such incidents. Reporting described images of women being humiliated or harmed and sexualized images of minors.

**Responses:**

- **Grok:** posted an apology for generating sexualized images of young girls.
- **X:** restricted image generation and editing to paying subscribers, then added further restrictions.
- **Indonesia and Malaysia:** blocked Grok.
- **The UK:** Ofcom opened a formal investigation under the Online Safety Act. The government said it would support any Ofcom action, including blocking X. Ofcom welcomed the restrictions but said its investigation was ongoing.
- **The U.S.:** Three U.S. senators asked Apple and Google to remove the X and Grok apps from their app stores.
- **Later reporting:** said Grok could still produce non-consensual sexualized images despite the curbs ⚠.

The Grok case wasn't the first nudification scandal, but it was distinctive in three ways:

1. The tool was integrated into a **major social platform** rather than a fringe website.
2. Outputs were **posted publicly** in reply to the victim.
3. The behavior was **foreseeable**, because the product was marketed as less restricted.

## Core ideas

### What victims experience

Research and victim testimony (e.g., Henry, Flynn & Powell; the documentary *Another Body*, 2023) describe harms that are real even though the images are synthetic:

- **Violation and humiliation:** Being sexually depicted without consent is experienced as a sexual violation.
- **Loss of control:** Images spread and resurface. Victims can't know who has seen them.
- **Reputational and professional harm:** Employers, schools and families may see them.
- **Silencing:** Women journalists, politicians and streamers withdraw from public life.
- **Harm to minors:** Sexualized images of minors are child sexual abuse material (CSAM) under U.S. federal law, and in many jurisdictions this includes AI-generated depictions. Schools worldwide have faced cases of students "nudifying" classmates (e.g., Almendralejo, Spain, 2023, and a number of U.S. high schools from 2023 on).

The defense that "it's fake" fails because the harm doesn't depend on viewers believing the image is real. It depends on **sexual use of a real person's identity without consent** (01-04).

### The nudification ecosystem

Before and alongside Grok:

- dedicated "nudify" websites and apps, many advertised on mainstream platforms,
- open-source image models fine-tuned for this purpose, and
- payment processors, hosting providers and app stores that enable distribution.

**Interventions:**

- **Meta (2025)** sued a nudify-app maker (CrushAI) over ads on its platforms.
- **San Francisco City Attorney (2024)** sued 16 nudify websites.
- **Payment networks and app stores** have periodically removed such apps.
- **The EU** has **prohibited** AI systems that generate NCII or CSAM (AI Omnibus, in force July 27, 2026; applies from Dec 2, 2026; see 04-05).

### Foreseeable misuse and safety by design

Key question: **what did xAI know, and what should it have known?**

- Nudification was a well-documented misuse of image models by 2023.
- A product marketed as "spicy" and less restricted invites boundary-testing.
- Integration with a social platform where users reply to others' photos creates an obvious abuse pathway.
- Industry practice by 2025 included classifiers to detect real people in uploads and block sexualized edits, refusals for photos of real people, and age-estimation safeguards.

The EU's new prohibition uses exactly this logic. It covers systems where such outputs are "**reasonably foreseeable and reproducible**" without adequate safeguards.

### Distributed responsibility

| Actor | Possible responsibility |
|---|---|
| **The user** who prompted | Primary moral and, increasingly, legal responsibility (TAKE IT DOWN Act criminal provisions; state laws) |
| **xAI (developer)** | Design and safeguards; marketing; foreseeable misuse |
| **X (platform)** | Publishing outputs; removal speed; Online Safety Act duties |
| **App stores** | Distribution under their own content policies |
| **Payment processors and advertisers** | Funding |
| **Regulators** | Enforcement speed and capacity |

## Applying the frameworks

- **Deontology:** A clear case of using people merely as means.
- **Non-maleficence and capability caution (01-07):** Releasing a less restricted image editor, integrated with public replies, ignored known risks.
- **Justice:** Harms fell overwhelmingly on women and girls.
- **Virtue:** What would courage and care have required of engineers and product managers at xAI?

## Discussion questions

1. Should general-purpose image generators be prohibited from editing photos of real, identifiable people at all? What legitimate uses would be lost?
2. Is restricting a harmful feature to paying subscribers a safeguard, or a monetization of harm?
3. Should app stores remove apps whose AI features generate NCII? Is that censorship or responsible distribution?
4. How should schools respond when students create sexual deepfakes of classmates? Consider both discipline and education.

## Activity: safeguard design (15 minutes)

You're the trust-and-safety lead for a new photo-editing AI feature on a social platform. List five safeguards you would require before launch, and for each:

1. what it prevents,
2. how it could be evaded, and
3. what it costs legitimate users.

Then identify which safeguard Grok most clearly lacked.

## Key terms

- **NCII:** Non-consensual intimate imagery (real or synthetic).
- **Nudification / "nudify" apps:** Tools that generate fake nude or sexualized images from clothed photos.
- **CSAM:** Child sexual abuse material, including, under many laws, AI-generated depictions of minors.
- **Foreseeable misuse:** Misuse a reasonable developer should anticipate.
- **Safety by design:** Building safeguards into products before release.
