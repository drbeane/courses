# 08-06 — Documentation: Model Cards, Datasheets, and Impact Assessments

**Module:** 8 — Governance, Accountability, and Responsible Deployment
**Estimated time:** 40 minutes

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe the purpose and contents of model cards, datasheets, system cards and impact assessments.
2. Evaluate real documentation for completeness and usefulness.
3. Draft key documentation sections for your A5 dossier.

## Opening case: the system card that came late

When frontier labs release major models, they increasingly publish **system cards** documenting:

- capabilities,
- safety evaluations (including dangerous-capability tests, 07-09),
- known limitations, and
- mitigations.

In 2025, several labs faced criticism for releasing models **before** their system cards, publishing thin ones, or omitting evaluation details. For example, Google released Gemini 2.5 Pro weeks before its model card, and the card was criticized as sparse. California's SB 53 (effective January 2026) now requires large frontier developers to publish safety frameworks and transparency reports at deployment.

Documentation is only useful if it's **timely, complete and honest**.

## Core ideas

### Types of documentation

| Document | Origin | Documents | Primary audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Datasheet for datasets** | Gebru et al., 2018/2021 | Motivation, composition, collection process, preprocessing, uses, distribution and maintenance of a dataset | Data users; auditors |
| **Model card** | Mitchell et al., 2019 | Model details, intended use, factors, metrics, evaluation data, disaggregated results, ethical considerations, caveats | Developers; deployers; auditors |
| **System card** | Labs, 2023 on | Whole-system behavior including safeguards; red-team and dangerous-capability results | Public; regulators; researchers |
| **AI impact assessment / algorithmic impact assessment** | Canada's Directive on Automated Decision-Making (2019); AI Now (2018) | Context, affected people, risks, mitigations, consultation | Decision-makers; public |
| **DPIA** | GDPR Art. 35 | Privacy risks of processing | DPOs; regulators (02-10) |
| **FRIA** | EU AI Act Art. 27 | Fundamental-rights risks of deploying high-risk AI | Deployers; market-surveillance authorities |
| **Transparency reports** | Platforms; SB 53 | Aggregate data on incidents and enforcement | Public |

### Model card essentials (Mitchell et al.)

1. **Model details:** developer, date, version, type, license, contact.
2. **Intended use:** primary uses and users; **out-of-scope uses**.
3. **Factors:** relevant groups, instruments and environments.
4. **Metrics:** performance measures, thresholds and why they were chosen.
5. **Evaluation data:** datasets, motivation and preprocessing.
6. **Training data** (as much as possible).
7. **Quantitative analyses:** **disaggregated** by group and **intersectional**.
8. **Ethical considerations.**
9. **Caveats and recommendations.**

### Fundamental rights impact assessment (EU AI Act Art. 27)

Deployers that are public bodies, private entities providing public services, or deployers of certain high-risk systems (credit scoring, life and health insurance pricing) must, before first use, assess:

- the processes in which the system will be used,
- the period and frequency of use,
- the categories of people affected,
- the specific risks of harm to them,
- human-oversight measures, and
- measures if risks materialize, including complaint mechanisms.

They must notify the market-surveillance authority of the results. Obligations follow the high-risk timeline (Dec 2027 for Annex III under the Omnibus ⚠).

### Canada's Algorithmic Impact Assessment

Canada's federal Directive on Automated Decision-Making requires an **online questionnaire-based AIA** that scores impact levels I–IV and scales requirements (peer review, human involvement, explanation, notice) to the level. Completed AIAs are published. It's a practical, public model.

### Documentation failure modes

- **Box-checking:** Templates filled with boilerplate.
- **Selective disclosure:** Favorable metrics only, and aggregate rather than disaggregated results.
- **Stale documentation:** Not updated after model updates or drift.
- **Unreadable:** Too technical for affected people, or too vague for auditors.
- **Missing out-of-scope uses:** No warnings against foreseeable misuse.
- **Too late:** Published after deployment (opening case).
- **No consequence:** Documentation that doesn't change decisions.

### Documentation as an ethical practice

Documentation forces teams to **articulate assumptions** and **confront limitations**. Done well, it is a form of **honesty** and **accountability** (08-05). It also creates a record that can be used in audits and litigation, which can motivate both diligence and defensive vagueness.

## Applying the frameworks

- **Explicability:** Documentation is how explicability is delivered in practice.
- **Justice:** Disaggregated results reveal who the system fails.
- **Virtue:** Candor about limitations.

## Discussion questions

1. Should model cards and system cards be legally required for all commercially deployed models? Which sections are essential?
2. How detailed should public dangerous-capability evaluation results be? Is there a risk of publishing a "roadmap" for misuse?
3. Should impact assessments be published, as in Canada, or confidential?
4. Who should write an impact assessment: the deployer, an independent party, or affected communities?

## Activity: draft for A5 (25 minutes)

For your A5 system, draft:

1. a **model/system card summary** (one page): intended use, out-of-scope uses, key metrics with disaggregation, known limitations, and
2. an **impact assessment summary**: affected groups, top three risks with mitigations, oversight, and complaint/redress mechanism.

Mark every place where you **lack information** and explain how you would obtain it. Transparent treatment of missing information is part of the A5 rubric.

## Key terms

- **Datasheet / model card / system card:** Documentation of data / models / whole systems.
- **AIA / DPIA / FRIA:** Algorithmic / data protection / fundamental rights impact assessment.
- **Disaggregated evaluation:** Performance broken down by group.
- **Out-of-scope use:** A use the developer warns against.
