# 03-10 — Lab: Consequential Decision Review

**Module:** 3 — Algorithmic Bias in Consequential Decisions
**Estimated time:** 90 minutes
**Type:** Lab (supports A2)

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

1. Audit a hiring screen's outcomes by group using a simple table-based analysis.
2. Investigate the outcome definition, proxies and data suitability of a decision system.
3. Recommend changes, including an accommodation and appeal process, and justify them.

## Scenario

**Northfield Health Partners** (fictional) is a regional health system hiring 120 patient-access representatives a year. It receives about 4,000 applications annually. Last year it adopted **ScreenRight**, a vendor tool that scores résumés from 0 to 100, and auto-advances applicants scoring 60 or above to a phone screen.

ScreenRight's documentation says:

- It was trained on 6 years of Northfield's past hiring decisions (who was hired), plus an industry dataset.
- Features include years of experience, job titles, education, keywords, employment gaps, ZIP-code distance to the facility, and "career stability" (average tenure per job).
- The vendor's validation shows the score correlates (r = 0.31) with being hired.
- Applicants aren't told that AI screening is used.

You've been asked by Northfield's Chief People Officer to review the tool before the contract renews.

## Data

Outcomes for last year's 4,000 applicants (self-reported demographics, voluntary; 3,400 provided them):

| Group | Applicants | Advanced (score ≥ 60) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Women | 2,300 | 920 |
| Men | 1,100 | 506 |
| White | 1,700 | 765 |
| Black | 900 | 270 |
| Hispanic/Latino | 550 | 220 |
| Asian | 250 | 110 |
| Age 40+ | 1,000 | 250 |
| Under 40 | 2,400 | 1,080 |

Feature analysis from the vendor (summary only):

- The largest negative weights are on employment gap > 6 months, ZIP distance > 15 miles, and average tenure < 18 months.
- The largest positive weights are on prior title "Patient Access" or "Registration," a bachelor's degree, and the keyword "Epic."

## Tasks

### Part 1 — Outcome analysis (25 min)

1. Compute the **selection rate** for each group.
2. Compute the **impact ratio** for each group (its selection rate ÷ the highest selection rate in its category).
3. Which groups fall below the four-fifths (0.8) threshold?
4. What does this analysis **not** tell you? Consider qualification, intersectional groups, disability, the 600 non-reporters and statistical significance.

### Part 2 — Construct and proxy investigation (25 min)

1. **Label:** The model predicts *past hiring decisions*. What construct does Northfield actually care about? How might the label differ from that construct (03-01)?
2. **Features:** For each heavily weighted feature, identify:
   - its plausible job relevance,
   - which protected groups it may proxy for, and
   - whether a less discriminatory alternative exists.
3. **Data suitability:** What would you need to know about the 6 years of historical decisions?
4. **Validity:** Is r = 0.31 with *being hired* evidence that the tool predicts *job performance*?

### Part 3 — Recommendation (25 min)

Write a one-page memo to the Chief People Officer that:

1. summarizes your findings in plain language,
2. states whether to **renew, renew with conditions, or not renew**,
3. lists required changes, with at least one each on:
   - features (remove, modify or test),
   - threshold or human review,
   - notice to applicants,
   - accommodation for applicants with disabilities, and
   - appeal or reconsideration,
4. specifies what monitoring Northfield should do going forward, and
5. identifies the relevant legal considerations, dated and with jurisdiction noted (Title VII, ADEA, ADA; any applicable state or local law). Note: you are not giving legal advice.

### Part 4 — Reflection (15 min)

In 150 words: which ethical framework most shaped your recommendation, and what would someone using a different framework recommend instead?

## Key terms

- **Selection rate:** The share of a group advanced or approved.
- **Impact ratio:** A group's selection rate divided by the highest group's rate.
- **Four-fifths rule:** The EEOC heuristic flagging impact ratios below 0.8.
- **Construct validity:** Whether the label measures what the organization cares about.
