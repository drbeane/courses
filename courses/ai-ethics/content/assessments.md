**Core and optional lessons:** The course assigns the core lessons. Lessons marked Optional on the Lessons page are further reading and case studies. An assessment may still send you to one: for example, the credit union scenario in A2 draws on the optional lessons on lending and on insurance and housing.

**Workload target:** about 8–10 hours per week including lessons.

## A1. Ethical case reasoning and structured discussion (15%)

**Part 1 — Position statement (Week 1, 5%).** In 800–1,000 words, take a position on one assigned 2026 case from Lesson 01-01. Apply at least two frameworks from Module 1, state the strongest counterargument in its best form, and explain why your position survives it or how you would revise it.

**Part 2 — Four structured debates (Weeks 2, 3, 5, 7; 10% total).** Students are assigned a side, which they do not choose. Each debate has three parts: an opening post of 300 words max with at least two sources, a rebuttal of 200 words max, and a closing "what would change my mind" statement of 100 words max.

| Week | Debate resolution |
|---|---|
| 2 | Police should be permitted to use live facial recognition in public spaces under a statutory framework. |
| 3 | Employers should be prohibited from using AI to screen out job applicants without human review of every rejection. |
| 5 | Employers may monitor remote workers' keystrokes and screens if they disclose it in advance. |
| 7 | Frontier-capable model weights should not be publicly released. |

**Rubric (each debate):** accuracy and use of evidence (40%), engagement with the opposing side's best argument (30%), clarity (15%), dated and correctly characterized legal claims (15%).

## A2. Rights, data and governance case (20%)

**Due:** End of Week 3. Draws on Modules 2–3 and Lessons 01-08 and 01-09.

Students choose one of three supplied scenarios:

1. A city considers buying a facial recognition system for its police department.
2. A mid-size employer considers an AI resume-screening vendor.
3. A regional credit union considers an ML underwriting model using alternative data.

**Deliverable (2,000–2,500 words plus tables):**

1. **Stakeholder map:** Who is affected, how, and how much power each party has.
2. **Data-flow diagram:** What data is collected, from whom, where it goes and how long it is kept.
3. **Authority memo:** What laws, standards and policies apply, with an "as of" date for each, separating legal obligations from voluntary standards and ethical responsibilities.
4. **Contextual fairness and accessibility critique:** At least one fairness metric discussed, and its limits stated.
5. **Recommendation:** What you would require before purchase, and the benefit that would be lost if the answer is no.

**Rubric:**
- Completeness of stakeholders and data flows (20%)
- Accuracy and dating of legal authority (20%)
- Quality of fairness and privacy analysis (30%)
- Feasibility of the recommendation (20%)
- Writing (10%)

## A3. Societal-impact evidence brief (15%)

**Due:** End of Week 6. Draws on Modules 5–6.

Choose one: (a) a company's plan to automate a customer-service department, (b) a proposed 300 MW data center in a water-stressed county, or (c) adoption of productivity-monitoring software at a remote-first firm.

**Deliverable (1,500–2,000 words):** A brief for a decision maker that:

- Synthesizes at least six credible sources, at least three of them primary data or peer-reviewed.
- Labels every claim as **measured**, **estimated**, **forecast** or **speculative**.
- Compares benefits and burdens across at least three affected groups, including workers or community members, not just the organization.
- Ends with a recommendation, the benefit that would be lost if the answer is no, and the evidence that would change it.

**Rubric:**
- Evidence quality and labeling (35%)
- Distributional analysis (25%)
- Honesty about uncertainty (20%)
- Recommendation (20%)

## A4. Failure investigation and safeguard evaluation (20%)

**Due:** End of Week 7. Builds on Labs 04-10 and 07-10.

Choose one generative or agentic system, either a supplied simulated assistant or a public tool used only on harmless tasks. Design and run a **bounded** test protocol of 15–30 test cases targeting one risk:

- fabricated citations or facts,
- unsafe compliance with instructions embedded in retrieved content (simulated only),
- inconsistent handling across demographic variants of the same request, or
- privacy leakage of supplied synthetic personal data.

**Deliverable:**

1. **Protocol:** What you tested and why, how cases were constructed, and your success criteria.
2. **Results table:** All cases, with raw outputs in an appendix.
3. **Failure analysis:** What failed, what the likely mechanism is, and who would be harmed in deployment.
4. **Safeguards:** At least two proposed safeguards. Re-test at least one, and state what each safeguard **cannot** do.
5. **Limits:** Why your sample does not prove the system safe or unsafe.

**Prohibited:** testing on live systems you do not own with injected payloads; generating sexual, violent or harassing content about real people; using real personal data.

**Rubric:**
- Protocol design (25%)
- Execution and documentation (20%)
- Failure analysis (25%)
- Safeguard evaluation (20%)
- Limitations (10%)

## A5. Final deployment decision portfolio and defense (25%)

**Due:** Week 8 (Lessons 08-09 and 08-10).

Students may continue the A2 scenario, the A4 system, or a new approved use case. Building new AI is **optional**. Evaluating an existing system is equally valued.

**Minimum dossier contents:**

1. Purpose, affected population and stakeholder map, with comparison to a non-AI or lower-automation alternative.
2. System, data and vendor context; known limitations; and transparent treatment of missing information.
3. Ranked harms and benefits, with mechanisms, affected groups, evidence and uncertainty for each.
4. An evaluation protocol and results, including at least one failure example and what changed because of it.
5. Safeguards tied to specific risks, with owners, residual limitations and monitoring triggers.
6. Data handling, a rights and obligations analysis (dated), and an accessible path to human review or redress.
7. A justified **deploy / restrict / do-not-deploy** recommendation, with its assumptions, the cost of not deploying, and the conditions for reconsideration.

**Defense:** A 10-minute recorded presentation followed by written answers to two reviewer questions.

**Rubric:**
- Evidence and evaluation (30%)
- Harms/benefits and distribution (20%)
- Safeguards and accountability (20%)
- Recommendation and conditions (15%)
- Defense (15%)

A well-reasoned **do-not-deploy** recommendation can earn full marks. A polished demo with unsupported safety claims cannot.

## A6. Independent peer review (5%)

Each student reviews one classmate's A5 dossier using the A5 rubric. The review must:

- identify the two weakest evidentiary claims,
- propose one missing stakeholder or harm, and
- say whether the recommendation follows from the evidence.

The author responds in 200 words or fewer. Reviews are graded on specificity and constructiveness, not on severity.

## Academic integrity and AI use

Students may use AI tools for brainstorming, for searching (verifying everything found), for editing their own prose and for running test protocols, and must disclose all such use in a short AI-use statement on each deliverable. They may not submit AI-generated analysis as their own reasoning. In A4, the AI system is the object of study, so its outputs must be quoted and attributed.

## Accessibility and well-being

Several cases involve sexual imagery, suicide, wrongful arrest and abuse. Lessons flag these in advance. Students may request an alternative case for any assessment without explanation. No activity requires disclosing personal experiences.
