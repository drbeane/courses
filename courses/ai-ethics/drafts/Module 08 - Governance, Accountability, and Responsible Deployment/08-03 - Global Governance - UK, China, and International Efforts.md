# 08-03 — Global Governance: UK, China, and International Efforts

**Module:** 8 — Governance, Accountability, and Responsible Deployment
**Estimated time:** 35 minutes

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Compare AI governance approaches in the UK, China and other jurisdictions with the EU and the U.S.
2. Describe international AI governance efforts: treaties, summits, safety institutes and scientific reports.
3. Evaluate whether international coordination on AI is feasible and desirable.

## Opening case: from Bletchley to Paris and beyond

- **November 2023:** The UK hosted the first AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park. Twenty-eight countries, including the U.S. and China, plus the EU, signed the **Bletchley Declaration**, recognizing risks from frontier AI and committing to cooperation.
- **May 2024 (Seoul):** Sixteen companies signed voluntary "Frontier AI Safety Commitments" to publish safety frameworks.
- **February 2025 (Paris):** The renamed **AI Action Summit** shifted emphasis toward innovation and inclusion. Sixty-plus countries signed a declaration on inclusive and sustainable AI. **The U.S. and UK declined to sign.** U.S. Vice President Vance warned against "excessive regulation."
- **February 2026:** India hosted the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi ⚠.

The trajectory, from safety-focused cooperation to competition-focused divergence, illustrates how fragile international AI governance is.

## Core ideas

### Comparative approaches

| Jurisdiction | Approach (as of October 2026 ⚠) |
|---|---|
| **EU** | Comprehensive, risk-based, rights-grounded statute (08-01) |
| **U.S.** | Sectoral, enforcement-based, innovation-focused federal policy; active states; preemption conflict (08-02) |
| **UK** | "Pro-innovation," principles-based. Existing regulators apply five principles (safety, transparency, fairness, accountability, contestability) without a general AI statute. The AI Safety Institute was renamed the **AI Security Institute** (Feb 2025). Sector rules include the Online Safety Act (04-04) and planned statutory facial-recognition rules (02-05). A frontier AI bill was repeatedly signaled but not enacted ⚠. |
| **China** | Targeted, iterative regulations: algorithm recommendation (2022), deep synthesis (2023), generative AI interim measures (2023), and AI-content labeling (Sept 2025). Algorithm filing and registry with the Cyberspace Administration. Security assessments. Content must reflect "core socialist values." Strong state control alongside promotion of the domestic AI industry and open-weight releases. |
| **Canada** | The proposed AI and Data Act died with Parliament's prorogation (Jan 2025). Voluntary code for generative AI ⚠. |
| **Japan** | AI Promotion Act (May 2025): a light-touch, promotional framework ⚠. |
| **South Korea** | AI Basic Act (Jan 2025, effective Jan 2026): a risk-based framework for "high-impact" AI ⚠. |
| **Brazil** | AI bill (PL 2338) advancing ⚠. |
| **African Union** | Continental AI Strategy (2024); national strategies in Kenya, Nigeria, Rwanda and elsewhere. |

### Different values, different rules

Comparisons reveal different priorities:

- **The EU:** fundamental rights and market integration.
- **The U.S.:** innovation, competition with China, free expression and (recently) deregulation.
- **China:** social stability, state control of information, and industrial development.
- **The UK:** positioning as a pro-innovation hub with safety expertise.

The same technology, such as content labeling, can serve different ends. China's labeling rules support content control, while the EU's support user autonomy.

### International instruments

| Instrument | Nature | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **OECD AI Principles** (2019, updated 2024) | Non-binding | 47 adherents |
| **UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI** (2021) | Non-binding | 193 member states |
| **G7 Hiroshima Process** (2023) | Guiding principles and code of conduct for advanced AI | Voluntary; reporting framework (2025) |
| **Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI** (opened Sept 2024) | **First legally binding** international AI treaty, covering human rights, democracy and the rule of law | Signed by the EU, UK, U.S. (under the prior administration), Israel and others; ratifications pending ⚠ |
| **UN General Assembly resolutions** (2024) | Non-binding | U.S.-led and China-led resolutions both adopted |
| **UN Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and Global Dialogue on AI Governance** (established 2025) | Advisory | Launched ⚠ |
| **International Network of AI Safety Institutes** (2024) | Technical cooperation | Members include the U.S., UK, EU, Japan, Korea, Singapore, Canada, France, Kenya and Australia |
| **International AI Safety Report** (2025, 2026) | Scientific synthesis (Bengio-led, 30+ countries) | Published |

### Is international coordination feasible?

**Reasons for optimism:**

- Shared interests in avoiding catastrophic misuse (bioweapons, cyber).
- Precedents: nuclear non-proliferation, aviation safety (ICAO) and climate (IPCC as a model for scientific panels).
- Technical cooperation among safety institutes.
- Industry's interest in interoperable rules.

**Reasons for skepticism:**

- U.S.–China strategic competition (export controls, 07-03).
- Divergent values (rights vs. control vs. innovation).
- AI is diffuse and dual-use, which makes verification hard. Compute governance has been proposed as a verifiable chokepoint.
- Political volatility: commitments made at Bletchley were weakened by Paris.

## Applying the frameworks

- **Global justice (06-06):** Whose voices shape global AI rules? The Global South has often been underrepresented.
- **Consequences:** Uncoordinated regulation may produce "races to the bottom," or useful experimentation.
- **Rights:** Is a rights-based treaty meaningful without major powers' ratification?

## Discussion questions

1. Should there be an "IAEA for AI"? What would it inspect, and could it work?
2. Is China's targeted, iterative approach more effective than the EU's comprehensive one? By what measure?
3. Why do you think the U.S. and UK declined to sign the Paris declaration? Were they right?
4. How should the Global South be represented in AI governance?

## Activity: governance comparison (15 minutes)

Pick one issue (deepfake labeling, facial recognition or frontier model safety). Compare how the EU, U.S., UK and China handle it, in a 4-row table:

- the rule,
- the binding status,
- the enforcer, and
- the underlying value.

## Key terms

- **Bletchley Declaration:** The 2023 international statement on frontier AI risks.
- **Framework Convention on AI:** The Council of Europe's binding AI treaty.
- **AI Security Institute (UK) / CAISI (U.S.):** Government bodies evaluating frontier AI.
- **Compute governance:** Regulating AI through control of computing resources.
