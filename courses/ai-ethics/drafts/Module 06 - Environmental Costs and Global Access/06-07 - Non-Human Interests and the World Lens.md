# 06-07 — Non-Human Interests and the World Lens

**Module:** 6 — Environmental Costs and Global Access
**Estimated time:** 35 minutes

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Apply the decency framework's non-human and world lenses to AI (carried over from COSC-643).
2. Analyze AI's effects on animals and ecosystems, both harmful and beneficial.
3. Reason about obligations to future generations in AI decisions.

## Opening case: listening to whales

Project CETI (Cetacean Translation Initiative), launched in 2020, uses machine learning to analyze sperm whale vocalizations ("codas"). In 2024, researchers reported in *Nature Communications* that sperm whale codas have a combinatorial structure, a "phonetic alphabet," richer than previously known. Similar AI projects study elephants, bats and birds (e.g., the Earth Species Project).

At the same time, AI-driven expansion of data centers, mining and energy infrastructure destroys habitat. AI enables industrial fishing optimization and factory-farm automation.

AI can extend moral attention to non-human beings, or intensify their exploitation.

## Core ideas

### Why non-humans count

COSC-643 applied Todd May's decency lens to "non-humans" because many ethical frameworks extend moral consideration beyond humans:

- **Utilitarianism (Bentham, Singer):** "The question is not, Can they reason? nor, Can they talk? but, Can they suffer?" Sentient animals' suffering counts.
- **Rights views (Regan):** Animals that are "subjects of a life" have inherent value.
- **Environmental ethics (Leopold's land ethic):** "A thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability, and beauty of the biotic community."
- **Relational and Indigenous perspectives:** Humans exist in relationships of reciprocity with land, water and other beings.

### AI's effects on non-humans

| Harmful | Beneficial |
|---|---|
| Habitat loss from data centers, mines and transmission lines | Biodiversity monitoring (camera traps, acoustic sensors, eDNA analysis) |
| Water withdrawals affecting aquatic ecosystems | Anti-poaching patrol optimization (e.g., PAWS) |
| Precision livestock farming that intensifies confinement | Disease detection in wildlife |
| Optimizing industrial fishing | Illegal fishing detection via satellite (Global Fishing Watch) |
| E-waste and toxic contamination | Species identification (iNaturalist, Merlin) |
| Light and noise pollution from facilities | Animal communication research (CETI) |

**Precision livestock farming:** AI monitoring of individual animals' health and behavior can improve welfare (early disease detection), or be used to increase stocking density and productivity in ways that worsen welfare. The same technology serves different values.

### Future generations

Many AI decisions have long-lasting effects:

- emissions persist in the atmosphere for centuries,
- infrastructure locks in for decades (06-03),
- institutional and political arrangements around AI may be hard to reverse, and
- some argue frontier AI poses risks to humanity's long-term future (07-09).

**Positions on obligations to future people:**

- **Strong:** Future people count equally. Since there may be vastly more of them, their interests may dominate (longtermism).
- **Moderate:** Future people count, but uncertainty and discounting reduce the weight of distant effects.
- **Critiques:** Longtermism can justify neglecting present, measurable harms in favor of speculative futures (01-03). Present injustice affects future generations too.

### The world lens

May's "world" lens asks about effects on society, the planet and the shared conditions of life:

- the climate system,
- the information ecosystem (Module 4),
- democratic institutions,
- global stability and peace, and
- the conditions for future human and non-human flourishing.

## Applying the frameworks

- **Consequentialism (expanded):** Include non-human suffering and ecosystem effects in cost–benefit analysis.
- **Justice (intergenerational):** Rawls's "just savings principle," that each generation should preserve conditions for the next.
- **Virtue:** Care and humility toward the more-than-human world.
- **Decency:** Giving reasonable weight to interests we usually ignore.

## Discussion questions

1. Should environmental impact assessments for data centers include effects on wildlife and ecosystems, not just humans?
2. If AI could enable two-way communication with animals, what ethical obligations would follow?
3. Is it consistent to worry about AI's carbon footprint while ignoring its use in factory farming?
4. How much weight should decisions today give to people living in 2100?

## Activity: lens application (15 minutes)

Choose one AI deployment from earlier modules. Write a paragraph applying **only** the non-human and world lenses. What do these lenses reveal that the self and others lenses missed?

## Key terms

- **Sentience:** The capacity to have experiences, including suffering.
- **Land ethic:** Leopold's ethic of the biotic community.
- **Longtermism:** The view that positively influencing the long-term future is a key moral priority.
- **Just savings principle:** Rawls's principle of obligations to future generations.
- **Precision livestock farming:** Technology-based monitoring and management of individual farm animals.
