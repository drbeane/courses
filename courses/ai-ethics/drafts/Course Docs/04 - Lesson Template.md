# Lesson Template

Lessons do not all share one structure (decided 2026-11-15). Every lesson has three fixed elements, and the content picks one of six lesson types for everything in between. The v1 lessons still follow the old nine-section structure until they are rewritten.

## Fixed elements (every lesson)

```markdown
# MM-LL — Lesson Title

**Module:** N — Module Title
**Type:** System in the world | Idea | How it works | Rules | Case study | Lab
**Estimated time:** XX minutes
**Content note:** (only if the lesson discusses sexual abuse, suicide, violence, etc.)

**In this lesson:** one sentence on what the lesson covers and why it matters.

…the body, shaped by the lesson type…

## Check your understanding
Three or four quick questions with answers.

## Sources
One reading marked **Core**; the rest optional. Dated sources. Legal or policy facts carry an "as of" date. Items marked ⚠ need re-verification before each offering.
```

- Define each key term in bold where it first appears. Terms are collected in the site glossary; there is no Key terms section.
- Link to related lessons and assessments in the text where they are relevant; there is no Connections section.
- Most lessons end the body with **Your turn**: one prompt, either a discussion question or a short task, not both.

## Lesson types

The shapes are loose. Section headings should name the idea, not the slot.

| Type | Use it for | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **System in the world** | A deployed system and its effects (most topical lessons) | The case, told as a story; the case for the system (what it is for, who gains, the evidence, in its strongest form); what goes wrong; where people disagree |
| **Idea** | Ethical theories and concepts (most of Module 1) | The idea; why it persuades; where it breaks; a case that tests it |
| **How it works** | Mechanisms (facial recognition, fairness metrics, what "open" means) | The mechanism; where it fails; why that matters ethically |
| **Rules** | Law, regulation and standards (most of Module 8) | What it says; who it binds; what it leaves open; as of when |
| **Case study** | A single incident that is the whole lesson | Timeline; what failed; who was responsible; what changed |
| **Lab** | Applied exercises and workshops | Scenario; tasks; deliverable |

## Style rules

- Write in connected prose. Use a table only for a real comparison and a list only for real steps or parallel items.
- Target about 2,000–2,500 words for a lesson; labs are as long as the tasks need.
- Present contested questions as contested. Give the strongest version of each side.
- Name an ethical framework only when it does work in the argument. Do not run every case through every framework.
- Label claims as measured, estimated, forecast or speculative when it matters.
- Date every legal status claim.
- Link each further-reading resource to its official publication, author manuscript, or institutional repository. Label access as free full text, free manuscript, paid/library, partial/preview, or unverified; an abstract or free sample is not the assigned full reading. Record versions and unresolved access in the [Reading Access Register](_Research/04%20-%20Reading%20Access%20Register.md).
- Use real cases where possible; label hypotheticals.
- Avoid graphic detail in sensitive cases. Describe harms at the level needed for analysis.
