## Opening case: the writers' and actors' strikes (2023)

In 2023, the Writers Guild of America (WGA) struck for 148 days and SAG-AFTRA (actors) for 118 days. AI was a central issue.

**WGA contract (September 2023):**

- AI can't write or rewrite literary material, and AI-generated material isn't considered source material, which protects writers' credit and pay.
- Writers may choose to use AI, but studios can't require it.
- Studios must disclose if materials given to writers were AI-generated.
- The WGA reserved the right to assert that exploiting writers' work to train AI is prohibited.

**SAG-AFTRA contract (November 2023):**

- informed consent and compensation for creating and using "digital replicas" of performers,
- limits on "synthetic performers," with notice to the union, and
- later agreements extended protections to video games after a 2024–2025 strike ⚠.

These contracts were among the first major collective agreements governing generative AI. They show workers using **bargaining power** to shape how AI is adopted, not just whether.

## Core ideas

### Worker voice mechanisms

| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| **Collective bargaining** | WGA/SAG-AFTRA; the Communications Workers of America on call-center AI; National Nurses United on clinical AI |
| **Works councils / co-determination** | Germany; EU AI Act requirement to inform worker representatives about high-risk AI |
| **Consultation mandates** | EU Platform Work Directive; proposed UK requirement (05-05) |
| **Framework agreements** | AFL-CIO–Microsoft partnership (Dec 2023) on AI and worker input |
| **Professional bodies** | Medical, legal and engineering standards (05-08) |
| **Individual rights** | Notice, access and contest rights (California CCPA/ADMT; NY proposals) |

### The policy menu

| Goal | Options | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cushion transitions** | Unemployment insurance modernization; wage insurance; retraining (Trade Adjustment Assistance as a model) | Evidence on retraining effectiveness is mixed. The best programs are sectoral and employer-linked. |
| **Protect job quality** | Bossware limits (05-05); quota laws (05-04); algorithmic management rules (05-06) | |
| **Preserve entry paths** | Apprenticeship incentives (05-02) | |
| **Share gains** | Tax policy; profit sharing; employee ownership (05-03) | |

### The role of AI developers

Some AI companies have published research on labor effects (e.g., the Anthropic Economic Index; OpenAI economic research) and funded economic-policy work. Ask:

- Is this research independent?
- Do developers have obligations beyond research, such as funding transitions or designing for augmentation?

### Your role as a technologist

Graduates of this program will build and deploy AI in workplaces. Choices that matter:

- automating tasks vs. augmenting people,
- involving affected workers in design,
- building monitoring proportionately, if at all,
- designing for skill growth, not deskilling, and
- raising concerns through legitimate channels.

## Applying the frameworks

- **Justice (procedural):** Those affected by workplace AI should have a voice in its adoption.
- **Consequences:** Bargaining may slow adoption but improve outcomes and acceptance.
- **Decency, others lens:** Designing with workers rather than for them.
- **Structural injustice:** Responsibility for transitions is shared among firms, governments, developers and workers.

## Discussion questions

1. Should unions be able to veto AI adoption in their workplaces? Negotiate its terms? Only be consulted?
2. The WGA contract protects writers from being required to use AI. Is that protection good for writers in the long run?
3. Which policy response would you prioritize for the next five years, and why?
4. What responsibility do AI developers have for the labor effects of their products?

## Key terms

- **Collective bargaining:** Negotiation between employers and worker representatives.
- **Digital replica:** An AI-generated likeness of a performer's voice or image.
- **Wage insurance:** Payments to displaced workers who take lower-paying jobs.
- **Sectoral training:** Job training designed with employers in a specific industry.
