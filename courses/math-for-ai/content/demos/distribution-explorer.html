What to notice

  1. The height of the curve is not a probability. A narrow uniform distribution has a density above 1. Only areas are probabilities, and the total area is always 1.
  2. Shrink the interval to a single point and the area goes to 0. A continuous random variable has probability 0 of equaling any one exact value.
  3. For any normal distribution, about 68% of the area lies within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2, and about 99.7% within 3. Change μ and σ and press those buttons again: the percentages do not move.
  4. Changing μ slides the normal curve without changing its shape. Raising σ spreads it out and lowers the peak, because the area has to stay 1.
  5. The exponential distribution is lopsided. More than half of its area lies below the mean, so "below the mean" is about 63%, not 50%.
  6. The share of samples that fall in the interval estimates the area. Draw more samples and the estimate tightens, which is Monte Carlo sampling again.

Normal probabilities here use a standard numerical approximation that is accurate to about six decimal places.
