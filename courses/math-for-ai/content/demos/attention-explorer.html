Weights for every token at once

Each row holds the weights one token gives to every token. Each row sums to 1. The whole table comes from one matrix product, followed by a softmax of each row.

What to notice

  1. The score between two tokens is the dot product of their vectors. It is large when the vectors are long and point the same way, near 0 when they are perpendicular, and negative when they point in opposite directions.
  2. Softmax turns the scores into weights that are positive and sum to 1. The output is the average of the vectors, each counted according to its weight, so it always lies among them.
  3. Choose "it". Its vector points nearly the same way as the vector for "trophy", so it gives "trophy" a large weight and its new vector moves toward it. Now drag "it" toward "suitcase" and watch the weights switch.
  4. A token usually gives a large weight to itself, because a vector always points the same way as itself.
  5. Divide the scores by a large number and the weights even out, so every token gets a plain average of all the vectors. Divide by a small number and each token attends to a single token.
  6. Nothing here was learned. In a real model, the vectors are multiplied by weight matrices before the dot products are taken, and training adjusts those matrices so that the useful tokens get the high scores.
