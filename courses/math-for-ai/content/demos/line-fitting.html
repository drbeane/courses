What to notice

  1. Each error is the vertical gap between a point and the line: the true value minus the prediction. SSE squares every gap and adds them up.
  2. The total area of the squares is the SSE. One far-off point makes one large square, which is why a single outlier can pull the best line toward it. Drag a point far away to see it.
  3. Changing the slope alone rarely reaches the minimum. The slope and intercept have to be tuned together, which is what the formula for the best coefficients does in one step.
  4. r² compares your SSE with the SSE of a flat line at the mean of y. A value near 1 means the line explains most of the variation. A negative value means your line is worse than the flat line.
  5. The best line always passes through the point (mean of x, mean of y).
