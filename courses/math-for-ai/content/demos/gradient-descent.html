What to notice

  1. Each step is the learning rate times the gradient. Far from the bottom the gradient is large and the steps are long. Near the bottom the gradient shrinks toward 0, so the steps shrink too, even though α never changes.
  2. On "One valley", try α = 0.1, then 0.9, then 1.1. A small rate crawls. A large rate jumps across the valley and bounces. Past α = 1 every jump lands farther away than the last, and the values blow up.
  3. On "Two valleys", start on the right side, then on the left. The algorithm rolls into whichever valley is nearer. It finds a local minimum, with no way to know whether a deeper one exists.
  4. On "Narrow valley", the gradient points across the valley much more than along it. With α near 0.2 the path zigzags from wall to wall and makes slow progress along the floor.
  5. Start exactly on a flat spot, such as the top of a hill, and the gradient is 0. The algorithm does not move at all.
  6. Switch to ascent and the same rule climbs instead: the only change is the sign in the update.
