What to notice

  1. The two columns of A are where the points (1, 0) and (0, 1) land. Once you know those two arrows, you know where every point goes.
  2. Straight grid lines stay straight and evenly spaced, and the origin never moves. That is what makes the transformation linear.
  3. The shaded shape is the image of the unit square. Its area is the size of the determinant. A negative determinant means the plane has been flipped over.
  4. Choose "Singular". The determinant is 0, the whole plane collapses onto one line, and different points land on the same spot. Nothing can undo that, so the matrix has no inverse.
  5. Drag x onto a dashed line. Ax then lies on the same line as x: the matrix only stretches it, by the eigenvalue. That is the equation Ax = λx.
  6. Choose "Rotate 45°". Every point turns, so no direction is kept, and the matrix has no real eigenvalues.
